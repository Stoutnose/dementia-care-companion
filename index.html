Looking after yourself

Caring for someone is hard work. Take regular breaks, accept offers of help, and look for a local caregiver support group, respite care or day program. If you feel exhausted, low or short-tempered most days, see your own doctor. This is very common, and help works.

Call the doctor soon if you notice

  • Sudden confusion, drowsiness or a change in behaviour over hours or days. This is often an infection, dehydration, constipation, pain or a new medicine.
  • A fall, especially with a knock to the head or if they take blood thinners
  • Fever, a new cough, or breathing that is faster or harder than usual
  • Coughing or choking when eating or drinking
  • Not eating or drinking, weight loss, or sudden weight gain or swollen ankles
  • A red, painful or broken area of skin, especially on the heels, hips or lower back
  • New hallucinations, low mood, or agitation that puts anyone at risk

Call the emergency number now for

  • Chest pain, severe breathlessness, or blue or grey lips
  • Signs of stroke: face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech
  • A seizure lasting more than 5 minutes, or not waking up afterwards
  • Unconsciousness, or very low blood sugar that does not improve
